1. INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE OF OPINION

1.1 We have acted as legal counsel to Terminal Investment Limited Holding S.A. (“TIL”) and the Concessionaire Consortium (together with Vietnam Maritime Corporation (VIMC) and Saigon Port Joint Stock Company) in connection with the Concession Agreement for the Can Gio International Transshipment Port (the “Concession Agreement”).

1.2 This opinion addresses the validity, enforceability, and compliance of the proposed tokenized ownership structure (the “Tokenized Structure”) through which accredited investors may obtain indirect beneficial ownership interests in the Project.

1.3 We have examined the following documents:

  • The Concession Agreement (Agreement No. MOT‑CG‑2026‑001, dated April 2026) and all Schedules;
  • Investment Registration Certificate issued by the Ho Chi Minh City People’s Committee (No. IRC‑2026‑089);
  • The Tokenized Asset Structure Memorandum (dated January 2026);
  • Form of Investor Purchase Agreement and Digital Securities Framework;
  • Relevant provisions of the Vietnamese Civil Code (2015), Securities Law (2019), Maritime Code (2015), and related implementing regulations.

All documents reviewed are originals or certified copies, and we have assumed their authenticity and completeness as of March 1, 2026.

2. SUMMARY OF CONCLUSIONS

2.1 Validity of Tokenized Structure

Based on our analysis, the Tokenized Structure constitutes a legally valid and enforceable form of indirect ownership under Vietnamese law. Each token represents a proportionate beneficial interest in the underlying physical port infrastructure assets, with the structure operating as a recognized form of “other property rights” under Article 105 of the Civil Code.

2.2 Regulatory Compliance for Accredited Investors

The offering of tokens to accredited investors qualifies for the private placement exemption under Article 32 of the Securities Law (2019), provided that: (i) each investor meets the accredited investor criteria as defined in Decree No. 155/2020/ND‑CP; (ii) the total number of investors does not exceed 100 qualified investors; and (iii) no general solicitation or public advertising is used.

2.3 Asset‑Backed Security Status

The tokens are classified as “securities” under Article 4, Section 2 of the Securities Law, which defines securities to include “other types of securities as prescribed by the government.” The State Securities Commission has not yet issued specific regulations for digital securities, but the current legal framework permits their treatment as asset‑backed securities through a special purpose vehicle (SPV) structure.

3. LEGAL FRAMEWORK ANALYSIS

3.1 Civil Code (2015)

Articles 105‑108 of the Civil Code recognize property rights as including ownership rights and “other property rights.” The Tokenized Structure falls within the scope of “other property rights,” as each token confers a direct beneficial interest in the Project’s revenue streams and, indirectly, in the infrastructure assets. The SPV established for the tokenized offering acts as the legal owner of the economic interest, with token holders as beneficial owners.

3.2 Securities Law (2019)

The private placement exemption under Article 32 allows offerings to “professional securities investors” (accredited investors) without public registration. Decree No. 155/2020/ND‑CP defines accredited investors as individuals with net assets exceeding VND 5 billion (approx. US$200,000) or financial institutions. The TILGroup offering complies with these thresholds. However, we note that digital securities are not explicitly mentioned; the legal characterization relies on the “other types of securities” catch‑all provision. Pending specific digital asset legislation (expected by 2027), we consider the current framework sufficient but subject to future regulatory development.

3.3 Maritime Code (2015)

Article 76 of the Maritime Code permits foreign investment in maritime infrastructure through concession agreements. The Tokenized Structure does not alter the concession rights granted to the Concessionaire Consortium; it merely provides a mechanism for third‑party investment participation. The Grantor (Ministry of Transport) has acknowledged the investment structure as consistent with the Concession Agreement, provided that the Concessionaire remains the direct counterparty to the Grantor.

4. RISK ASSESSMENT AND MITIGATIONS

4.1 Regulatory Risk – Evolving Digital Asset Framework

The National Assembly has announced its intention to consider comprehensive digital asset legislation by 2027. There is a risk that future regulations may impose licensing requirements, registration obligations, or restrictions that could affect the Tokenized Structure. Mitigation: We recommend that the Concessionaire proactively engage with the State Securities Commission and the Ministry of Finance, and structure the SPV to allow for adaptation (e.g., conversion to conventional securities if required).

4.2 Enforcement Risk – Judicial Interpretation

Vietnamese courts have not yet ruled on the enforceability of tokenized ownership rights. In the absence of specific precedent, courts would likely rely on general contract and property law principles. Mitigation: The use of a Vietnamese SPV as the legal titleholder, combined with Vietnamese governing law and dispute resolution under the Concession Agreement (SIAC arbitration), provides the strongest enforcement position currently available.

4.3 Tax Risk

Tax treatment of tokenized securities is not explicitly addressed in current tax circulars. However, based on general principles, we expect that token holders will be treated similarly to holders of conventional asset‑backed securities for income tax purposes (i.e., income from securities is subject to withholding tax at applicable rates). Mitigation: The Concessionaire should seek a tax ruling from the General Department of Taxation.

5. ONGOING COMPLIANCE OBLIGATIONS

5.1 The Concessionaire and the SPV must maintain ongoing compliance with:

  • Anti‑Money Laundering (AML) regulations under State Bank of Vietnam Circular No. 09/2023/TT‑NHNN;
  • Know‑Your‑Customer (KYC) obligations for investor onboarding, including identity verification and beneficial ownership disclosure;
  • Tax reporting obligations under Circular No. 92/2015/TT‑BTC (as amended), including quarterly withholding and annual reporting of payments to token holders;
  • Annual audit requirements for the SPV’s financial statements, to be conducted by an independent auditing firm registered in Vietnam.

5.2 The Concessionaire shall provide token holders with annual disclosure documents, including audited financial statements of the Project and a report on the status of the concession, no later than 90 days after the end of each fiscal year.
